## Introduction-to-Conclusion consistency

The Introduction asks:

> How does the apparent utility of heterogeneous molecular representations
> change when they are evaluated under controlled linear and nonlinear
> predictive probes using a frozen, reproducible ADMET benchmark?

The Conclusion answers exactly that question: representation performance
was probe-dependent; a compact physicochemical representation gave the
strongest nonlinear cross-endpoint ranking; that ranking was reproduced
under independent repartitioning; and no representation separated clearly
under the linear probe.

The Introduction promises **no** improved ADMET prediction, no new
architecture, no state-of-the-art comparison and no learned-representation
evaluation — each of which the manuscript would fail to deliver. It states
explicitly that MolFusion "is not a predictive model and is not offered as
an improved ADMET predictor", and that learned representations "were not
part of the benchmark and are not evaluated in this study". All four
exclusions are enforced by test.

The Abstract remains governed by **C1, C2, C3 only**; no claim was promoted
into it because later sections now exist.
